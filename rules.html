Setup

How to get from box to first turn. The sequence below follows current design intent — several steps reference mechanics that are still being finalized elsewhere in the rules.

  1. Assemble the board. Place the fixed board elements — Operations Center, The Dock, Airlock/Hull Access, Transit Infrastructure, corridors, vent network. See // 07 Board & Rooms.
  2. Place Room Tiles / fog of war. ~15–20 rooms start undiscovered. Exact placement procedure TBD.
  3. Choose characters. Each player selects one of the 5–6 playable characters. See // 05 Characters. Starting loadout per character TBD.
  4. Set starting resources & inventory. Starting Health, Speed, and any starting items TBD.
  5. Shuffle the Echo Deck and place it as the shared draw pile. See // 10 The Echo Deck.
  6. Set the Creature to DORMANT. It does not enter play until the Introduction Sequence triggers. See // 06 The Creature & Noise.
  7. Place the Objective Board and any starting Blueprint slots. See // 20 Objective Board.
  8. Determine turn order. Players choose who goes first and the order everyone takes their turn in. Fixed for the rest of the game — no rotating or changing later.

The Creature & Noise

The crew does not know what they are dealing with. They call it the Creature because they have no better word. It moves wrong. It appears where it should not. Weapons damage it but do not stop it. It is drawn to activity, to noise, to signal.

Creature States

StateDescription
DORMANTNot yet on board. Failed noise checks generate Threat Tokens only. Cannot activate until introduced.
ACTIVEOn the board. Full noise check rules apply. Position known to crew.
SIGNAL LOSTOn board but location unknown. Noise checks still fire. Position hidden until re-emergence.
VENTEDRetreated into ship infrastructure. Temporarily off board. Re-emerges via specific Echo cards.

Noise

Each player tracks their own Noise Tracker, 1–12, on their character board — it is not a shared meter. Resets at the start of that player's turn. Every Move and Action a player takes generates some amount of Noise on their own tracker — see // 12 Actions for the full cost of each. What that accumulated Noise does to the Creature is covered below.

Noise Check & Activation

// MechanicAt end of each player phase, roll D12 Noise Die vs that player's own accumulated Noise Level.
ResultOutcomeCreature Behavior
Roll > NoisePASSCreature moves HALF speed toward last noise location. Always moving — never fully still.
Fail · 1–4Direct PursuitFull speed directly toward last noise source.
Fail · 5–8Vent ShortcutUses speed to reach nearest vent (relative to creature) → emerges nearest vent to noise → continues with remaining speed.
Fail · 9–12Immediate VentInstant vent jump, no movement cost. Emerges at vent nearest noise source. Full speed toward noise.

Activation Tier Note

The activation tier (1–4, 5–8, 9–12) is determined by the number rolled on the D12 Noise Die — not by the noise level itself. High noise increases the probability of failure and expands dangerous possible outcomes, but does not guarantee any specific tier. The dice determine the outcome. The noise level determines the odds.

Line of Sight Rush

During creature movement, if the creature enters line of sight of any player at any point along its path, it rushes that player immediately — expending remaining movement. Non-discriminating: attacks any player in its path. Triggers on the first LoS contact during movement, not only at the end of the move.

Introduction Sequence

  • Dormant Phase: Failed noise checks produce Threat Tokens. Toggle cards inert.
  • Operations Center Objective: Crew reaches Operations Center, interacts with the main terminal → draws CONTACT card.
  • CONTACT Card: Names a room family. Threat Tokens stack on it. Card source and exact placement mechanic under reconsideration — see Open Items.
  • Introduction: Named room family discovered → creature placed in that room. Threat Tokens convert. Status flips to ACTIVE.
  • Scenario B: If family already on board when CONTACT drawn → creature emerges in most recently discovered room of that family.

Board & Rooms

The derelict vessel is a deep space research facility designed to be entirely self-sufficient — capable of sustaining its crew and operations indefinitely without resupply. This explains the breadth of room types and why the original crew could not simply escape.

Fixed Board Elements

  • Operations Center: ~60 spaces. Fixed position every game. The nerve center of the facility — main operations floor, Director's Station, observation deck. One dedicated remote terminal per room family. Higher noise, alarm risk. First objective: reach Operations Center and draw CONTACT card.
  • The Dock: Fixed on board edge. Escape ship berth. Houses 2×10 Fuel Grid.
  • Airlock / Hull Access: Fixed hull-adjacent locations. Main Airlock is primary crew entry. Emergency Airlock, Hull Breach Points, EVA Prep Bay also fixed. High danger zones.
  • Transit Infrastructure: Lift Lobbies, Rail Platforms, Conveyor Junctions. Fixed connectors between facility sections. Strategic chokepoints.
  • Corridors: Fixed, various shapes. No Echo cards. Vent access points printed here. Board designed so no sections can be sealed off by tile placement.
  • Vent Network: Printed on board in space between room/corridor boundaries. Traversable. 1–2 spaces wide. Creature-native but player-accessible.

Room Tiles

  • 5×6 footprint with asymmetrical notched corners.
  • Double-sided. Only one face physically fits a given board slot.
  • Player chooses one of two orientations — 4 total configurations per tile.
  • Door routing determines which fixed corridors connect to the room.

Room Internal Configurations

  • Open room: 4 doors, full navigation throughout.
  • Split room: Internal wall divides into 2 sub-rooms. Top/right doors form one. Bottom/left form another.
  • Elevated section: Upper and lower levels with ladder access. Some doors serve each level.
  • Single or three-door variants: Smaller rooms or restricted access configurations.

Turn Structure

A turn is one player's Player Phase immediately followed by a Creature Phase — not one Player Phase for the whole crew, then a single Creature Phase for the round. Each player triggers their own Creature Phase before play passes to the next player.

// Turn OrderPlayers choose who goes first and the order every player takes their turn in, at Setup. That order is fixed for the rest of the game — it does not change or rotate once play begins.

Player Phase

// Free-Form TurnEach player has 2 Moves and 2 Actions per turn. They may be taken in any order with one restriction — see callout below.
DescriptionNotes
Move 1 / Move 2Move up to Speed in any directionEntering an unexplored room → draw Echo Card. Moves may be taken before or after Actions in any order.
Action 1 / Action 2Any available actionFull list in // 12 Actions. Actions may be taken before or after Moves.
Character AbilityOptional. Once per turn.Any time during the phase. Does not replace an Action.
Track NoiseUpdate Noise MeterBefore Creature Phase begins.
// No Move After Action 2The player cannot take a Move after their second Action — with one exception: if Action 2 is Run or Quiet Move, one Move is permitted after. No other exception.

Creature Phase

Triggered once, immediately after the active player's Player Phase ends.

  1. Noise Check. Roll D12 Noise Die vs the active player's accumulated Noise Level (each player's Noise Tracker is their own). See // 06 The Creature & Noise for the full pass/fail table.
  2. Resolve Creature movement per the check's outcome — half speed toward the noise on a pass, or one of three failure tiers (Direct Pursuit, Vent Shortcut, Immediate Vent). See // 06.
  3. Apply Line of Sight Rush if the Creature gets LoS on any player at any point during that movement. See // 06.
  4. Resolve a Creature Encounter if the Creature reaches a player. See // 13 Combat.
  5. Noise resets to 0 for the next player's turn.

Roll Resolution

All D12 System Dice checks — card events, hazards, and skill checks — follow this structure. Modeled after Ship, Captain, Crew.

  • Start: Roll all 5 D12 System Dice. The card or check specifies which face(s) are required and how many.
  • Keep: Set aside any faces that count toward resolution. These are locked and cannot be re-rolled.
  • Re-roll: Roll remaining dice. Up to 3 rolls maximum per check.
  • VOID: A VOID face permanently removes that die from the pool for this roll sequence. It cannot be re-rolled and does not count toward any requirement.
  • WILD: Counts as any required face for the current roll's purpose.
  • Resolution: Requirements met when the specified faces and quantities are showing among kept dice. The roll is resolved at that point.

Noise on the Final Roll

  • Noise and Noise Spike faces count toward the noise meter only from the player's final roll — whichever roll they end on, whether that is roll 1, 2, or 3.
  • Noise faces from earlier rolls that are subsequently re-rolled do not carry over.
// Optional Clear RollOnce all required faces are satisfied, check the remaining dice — any that are not kept. If Noise or Noise Spike faces are showing among them, the player may choose to re-roll those specific dice using any remaining rolls. The goal is to land on a non-noise face and remove that noise from the final count. This is a genuine gamble: the re-roll may land on another Noise or Noise Spike, keeping or worsening the penalty. If no noise faces are showing after requirements are met, there is nothing to clear — no further rolls are available or needed. The optional clear roll is only possible when: (1) requirements are already satisfied, and (2) noise faces are still showing on non-kept dice.

Roll Resolution — Example

RollResultAction
Roll 11 of 3 required faces + 1 Noise + 3 othersKeep the 1 required face. Re-roll remaining 4 dice.
Roll 22 more required faces + 2 NoiseRequirements met (3/3). 2 Noise faces showing. Player may take Roll 3 to attempt clearing them.
Roll 3 (optional)1 Noise Spike + 1 non-noise faceFinal roll. 1 Noise Spike counts toward noise meter. Non-noise face ignored.

If the player had stopped at Roll 2, both Noise faces would have counted. By taking Roll 3, they cleared one but converted the other to a Noise Spike — a genuine gamble.

Room Card Tasks & Objectives

Not every Room Card carries a task, and not every task is an objective. Cards marked Objective feed the Objective Board (// 20) — their tasks are resolved in order, tied to that card's room family, and completed at whatever the task specifies (a room of that family, a terminal, or a workbench). Cards with a task that isn't marked Objective resolve immediately on draw and never touch the board.

Drawing Rules

  • Room Discovery: Enter unexplored room → draw Echo Card to populate it.
  • Family Cap: Max 2–3 rooms per family on board simultaneously.
  • Cascade Draw: If drawn card's family is at cap → resolve card, draw again. Each cascade is another Echo Event risk.
  • Terminal Draw: Interacting with terminal costs 1–2 actions and draws an Echo Card.
  • Late Game: Once all rooms discovered, terminals are the only way to draw cards.

Card Events & Hazards

Some Echo Cards trigger events or hazards requiring a D12 dice roll to resolve. Each card specifies the required face(s) and quantity needed. All resolutions follow the standard Roll Resolution rules — see // 09 The Dice Engine.

// NoteCard events and hazards use the D12 System Dice pool. Echo Event Mini-Games use the D6 Coordinate Dice. These are separate systems — do not mix them.

Operations Center Terminals

One dedicated terminal per room family. Available regardless of whether that room exists on board.

// WarningOperations Center terminals generate significantly higher noise than standard terminals. Failed interaction triggers an alarm — immediate noise spike of the card's stated noise value +3, plus a noise check before turn ends.

Echo Event Mini-Games

Interrupt the current action — room discovery or terminal interaction — and must fully resolve before play continues. No additional action cost. After resolution, original action continues.

Universal Wager Rules

  • Mandatory: Every Echo Event requires staking one resource before any dice are rolled.
  • No Resources: Automatic failure. +4 Noise immediately. No rolls taken. Continue to next card draw.
  • Donation: Crewmate may cover wager if physically present in the same room when event triggers.
  • Win: Wagered resource returned. Some mini-games award doubled payout.
  • Fail: Resource permanently lost. Noise penalty applied per mini-game.

Preparation Actions

// Ready Action

  • Cost: 1 action. Requires weapon equipped.
  • Applies to ranged and melee equally.
  • Effect: +1 pushback beyond weapon's normal cap.
  • Expires at end of creature phase if unused.

// Brace Action

  • Cost: 1 action. No weapon required.
  • Roll all 5 D12 system dice once.
  • Each Hazard or Wild face = −1 damage. Maximum −5.
  • Stacks with Armor and Defense Roll.

Defense Roll

  • Passive and automatic. No action or roll required.

Inventory & Equipment

Player Board Layout

  • Top: Character name, art, vital stats (Speed, Health tracker).
  • Left column: Equipment Grid (2×4 = 8 slots) — weapons and armor only.
  • Right column: Backpack Inventory Grid (~22 slots with compartments).
  • Bottom: Noise Tracker (1–12) and Quick Reference section.

Equipment Grid

  • 2×4 grid (8 slots). Ranged weapons, melee weapons, and armor — all housed here.
  • Weapons and armor do NOT compete with backpack inventory space.
  • Starting 1×1 weapon placed here at setup.

Backpack Inventory

  • ~22 slots in compartments. Compartments are cosmetic — items go anywhere they fit.
  • Ammo is stored in the backpack and competes for inventory space.

  • Certain compartments have designated item types (e.g., Fuel Cell slot).

  • Backpack boards are interchangeable between playthroughs.

Objective Board

Official term. Hosts the Blueprints — a set of static unlock slots — plus the Pending Objective Queue for objectives not yet slotted in. The crew already knows what these items are; the Blueprints represent figuring out how to actually assemble them from what's on hand aboard the Charon.

Blueprints Are Static Unlocks, Not Cards

Blueprints are never drawn. Each blueprint occupies a fixed slot on the Objective Board and specifies exactly how many objectives (0–3) it requires to unlock. That count lives on the blueprint slot only — objective cards never carry their own chain requirement, so a drawn objective can only ever occupy a single link in one blueprint's chain, never introduce a chain-within-a-chain.

Populating the Blueprints

  • Parallel by Default: Blueprints are independent — the crew can populate and work any blueprint's chain in any order they choose. No global order across them.
  • Free Placement: An objective drawn from a Room Card may be slotted into an eligible chain immediately, at no extra action cost — same action as the draw.
  • Explicit Prerequisites: A blueprint may name one specific prerequisite blueprint that must be fully completed before its own chain can be populated. This is a property of that one blueprint, not a rule about the Blueprints as a whole — see // 17 Inventory — Fuel Cells for the current example.
  • Ahead of Completion: On any blueprint without a prerequisite, population can run ahead of completion — every objective slot can be filled before that blueprint's own tasks are even started.
  • One Card, One Chain: Once an objective is slotted, it's locked to that chain — it can never be reapplied elsewhere.

Chain Resolution (In Order, Bottom-Up)

Everything resolves in order, at every level — it reads like a quest line: task unlocks task, objective unlocks objective, chain unlocks blueprint.

  • Tasks: Within a single objective, its own tasks (max 3) resolve in order — each must be completed before the next can be started. Tasks are completed at whatever the card specifies — a room of that objective's family, a terminal, or a workbench.
  • Objectives: Within a chain, objectives complete bottom-up — the objective furthest from the blueprint (the last one added to a full chain) must be fully completed before the next one up can be started. The final objective in the chain must complete before the blueprint's own tasks can begin.
  • Blueprints: Completing one blueprint has no effect on any other, unless that other blueprint explicitly names it as a prerequisite.

Example Task Types

  • Terminal Skill Check: Go to [family] room, interact with terminal, pass a D12 skill check with specific required faces — e.g. 2× Tech or 1× DNA.
  • Workbench Cost: Go to [family] room, interact with workbench, spend a specific resource cost — e.g. 2 Scrap + 1 Salvage.
  • Delivery: Carry specific item(s) to a [family] room and place them at the location the task specifies.
// NoteThese are illustrative examples, not an exhaustive task-type list — final task templates are part of card design.

Pending Objective Queue

An objective the crew doesn't slot immediately joins the back of the Pending Objective Queue. Pending objectives are pulled from the front of the queue only — players cannot cherry-pick a specific one out of order.

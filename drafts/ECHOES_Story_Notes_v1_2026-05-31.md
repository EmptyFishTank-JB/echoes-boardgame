### Space Travel — Speeds and Technology
Needs to be worked into the narrative naturally — not as a data dump, but through character context or a log entry. Key figures:

- **Distance:** Mars to derelict vessel ~28.48 AU (~4.26 billion km) at optimal alignment
- **Travel time:** 8 months (crew's vessel — older, heavier salvage ship, not cutting-edge)
- **Average speed:** ~729,000 km/h
- **Peak speed (mid-journey):** ~1,460,000 km/h (~0.00135c / 0.135% light speed)
- **Sustained acceleration:** ~0.004g (barely perceptible aboard)
- **Drive type:** Fusion drive — mature, standard technology by 2175
- **Old supply ships (2143 era):** ~120,000 km/h average / 3–5 year trip time
- **Standard civilian vessel (2175):** ~1,000,000–1,500,000 km/h average / ~5 months
- **The crew's vessel reads slow** — salvage ship, not a courier. 8 months is the conservative end of what 2175 drives can do. Hugo didn't charter them the fastest ship available.
- **Parker Solar Probe comparison** (for real-world grounding): hits ~690,000 km/h at perihelion as its peak. Crew's ship sustains ~729,000 km/h average for 8 months. Significant technological leap, plausible for 150 years of advancement.

**Suggested placement:** A brief crew conversation during the 8-month approach, or a data log on the vessel's bridge. Could be Zeke noting the speed differential between their drives and what the old supply ships ran. Feels most natural as dialogue rather than narration.

### The Escaped Pods — Survivors Question
15 of 20 pods launched. 5 remain (degraded, Bay 3). Of the 15 that launched:

- All beacons disabled before departure
- 32 years have passed
- Cryo systems rated for ~50 years at full power
- Some pods likely malfunctioned over 32 years — those crews are dead
- Some may still be intact — those crews are still in cryo, unknowing, with ~18 years of power remaining
- **No one knows they're out there.** Not the company. Not the crew. Not anyone.
- **Story beat:** The crew learns this in the pod bay. The emotional weight lands. The question of whether any survivors can be found or signaled is left open. Could become a mission objective if the crew restores broadcast capability.

## STARDATE FORMAT

`YYYY.DDD.HHMM`

- YYYY = Year
- DDD = Day of year (001–365/366)
- HHMM = 24-hour time

Examples:
- `2175.150.0213` = May 30, 2175 at 2:13 AM (crew arrival anchor)
- `2143.159` = Day 159 of 2143 (transmissions disabled)
- `2026.150.0213` = May 30, 2026 at 2:13 AM (real-world session start)

# ECHOES
## Prologue

---

### // SETTING

**The Derelict Vessel:** DSF Meridian — deep space research facility.
Self-sufficient. Designed for indefinite operation without resupply.
Vessel population at time of incident: approximately 150.
Core research crew: 14.

**The Incident:** 2143.
**New crew arrival:** 2175.
**Elapsed:** 32 years. 11,688 days.

---

### // CAST

**Boarding the derelict:**
- Jason Betancourt — BIM Systems Technician. Former mechanical designer. Institutional knowledge by sheer experience. The one Cesar turns to before making a call.
- Jaime Rodriguez — Electrical Specialist. Optimistic. Steadying presence. Long-time friend.
- Cooper "Cup" Gates — Infrastructure Tech. Youngest. Humor as armor. Earned his place.
- Ezekiel Tagle — Data Analyst. Quiet until he isn't. Already three steps ahead.
- Cesar Roque — Field Ops Lead. Speaks with confidence. Makes the call.

**Remaining aboard their vessel:**
- George Castañeda — Senior Comms Watch. Oldest. Easy-going. Falls asleep on duty. Good to have on the team.
- Maritza Garza — Mechanical Designer. Coasts. Fires back when needed.
- Lessly Ochoa — Mechanical Designer. Holds her own.
- Robert Tijerina — Project Manager. Forwards the directive. Name-drops Hugo. Means well enough.

**Not aboard — referenced only:**
- Hugo Avila — Mission Director. Client relations. Source of the directive.

---

## I. THE APPROACH

The forward viewport of their vessel filled with it slowly, the way bad things tend to — not all at once, but incrementally, until it was simply there and there was no pretending it wasn't.

The research facility. Dark. Enormous. No running lights, no beacon, no sign of cycle. A structure designed to sustain one hundred and fifty people indefinitely, sitting dead in the black like someone had simply walked away from it mid-sentence.

Jaime keyed the comms for the third time. Same tone as the first two — steady, professional, the kind of voice that didn't telegraph anxiety even when it had good reason to.

> **JAIME:** This is survey vessel on approach — please respond on standard or emergency channel. Registered salvage crew. Respond at your convenience.

Static. Long and flat.

> **COOPER:** "At your convenience." You're very polite to a dark ship, man.

> **JAIME:** You never know.

> **COOPER:** I feel like in this specific case we know.

Zeke was at the sensor array. He'd been quiet since they came into visual range, which meant he was actually thinking.

> **ZEKE:** Hull reads intact. Primary dock corridor is pressurized. Internal atmosphere — present. Most of the facility.

> **JAIME:** Most?

> **ZEKE:** A few sections aren't reading. Could be isolation seals. Could be something else. I'd need to be inside to tell the difference.

> **CESAR:** *(reviewing the schematics on his tablet)* Automated emergency broadcast should be transmitting regardless of crew status. It isn't. That's the part that doesn't fit.

> **COOPER:** So the backup plan also stopped. That's good. That's a great sign.

> **CESAR:** It isn't.

> **COOPER:** I know. I was being sarcastic.

> **CESAR:** I know. I was being accurate.

From the comms board, Robert came through — calm and measured in the particular way that meant he'd been sitting with something and decided to weigh in.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: What's the read from out there? Hugo's directive flagged automated systems as a priority check before we dock.

> **CESAR:** Emergency broadcast is inactive. Atmosphere and hull are nominal. We're assessing.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Copy. Jason — you've seen dark configurations before. Anything about this that concerns you?

> **JASON:** The broadcast being down is the only thing that doesn't add up. Everything else looks like a passive shutdown, not a catastrophic one. We won't know more until we're in.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Right, good. That's the expert read I was looking for.

George, who had been suspiciously quiet at the secondary comms board, shifted in his seat.

> **GEORGE:** I've got nothing on return signal. Still just noise.

> **COOPER:** Were you asleep?

> **GEORGE:** I was conserving energy. It's a long approach.

> **LESSLY** *(from somewhere behind him)*: He was asleep.

> **GEORGE:** *(not denying it)* I heard everything.

The docking alert chimed. Green across the board.

---

## II. THE CROSSING

The dock corridor between vessels was forty feet of pressurized tube. The facility's airlock accepted their override on the first attempt — no resistance, no alarm. It just opened.

Which was either good or the kind of bad that didn't bother announcing itself.

Emergency strips ran along the corridor floor — pale amber, low draw, just enough to keep your feet under you. The hum of active systems that every facility had, that constant low-frequency you stopped hearing after the first day — gone. The silence had texture to it.

> **COOPER:** *(quiet)* This is fine. Completely normal abandoned space facility.

Nobody replied. They moved.

---

## III. THE COMMAND CENTER

The Operations Center was wide and tiered, workstations arranged in arcs facing a primary display wall that had gone dark some time ago. At the center of the room, raised on a platform: the main terminal. One indicator light pulsed on its housing. Slow. Patient.

One hundred and fifty people had worked on this vessel.

None of them were here.

Cesar walked the perimeter first — exits, entry points, adjacencies. He did that in every room, automatically, like checking a site before signing off on it. The others spread into their natural positions: Jaime at a side workstation, trying for local logs. Zeke straight to the terminal. Jason taking in the room.

> **ZEKE:** It's active. Protected state, but the interface is live. It's been waiting for input.

> **CESAR:** What does it want?

> **ZEKE:** A user.

Cesar looked at Jason.

> **CESAR:** What's your read?

> **JASON:** Could be a standard idle lock. Touch it and it either opens a guest layer or asks for credentials. Either way we learn something. Could also be a trip — depends on how the system was configured when it went down.

> **CESAR:** *(a beat)* Zeke — try the guest layer. Don't push past any access prompts. Anything that asks for credentials, you stop and we read it first.

> **ZEKE:** Got it.

Comms crackled.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Status update. Hugo wanted a check-in before you interact with anything in the Operations Center.

> **CESAR:** We're at the main terminal. About to try guest access. Atmosphere is stable, room is clear.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Copy. Hugo flagged automated security as a concern — directive was not to trigger any lockout protocols.

> **CESAR:** We're aware.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Right, just — pass it along.

> **COOPER:** *(just to Jason, low)*: Every time.

> **JASON:** *(same volume)*: Every time.

Zeke touched the terminal.

The display came alive. Flickered. Resolved. Text populated line by line — deliberate, like something waking up and choosing its words.

```
ECHOES SHIP SYSTEM TERMINAL
v4.2.6

WELCOME BACK.

LAST SESSION:   11,688 DAYS AGO
LAST USER:      [REDACTED]
LAST INPUT:     [REDACTED]

PERSONNEL DETECTED:   5
AUTHORIZATION:        UNVERIFIED

INITIATING FACILITY STATUS REPORT...
```

> **JAIME:** *(watching the screen)* It's been counting days.

> **ZEKE:** It logged us the moment we crossed the airlock. Probably before.

The status report ran. Power levels, atmospheric data, system states — most of it nominal, some flagged in amber. Then:

```
PERSONNEL — ORIGINAL MANIFEST:   150
PERSONNEL — CURRENT:             [CLASSIFIED]
DISCREPANCY:                     LOGGED
CAUSE:                           ARCHIVED
ARCHIVE ACCESS:                  RESTRICTED — CLEARANCE REQUIRED
```

A long moment.

> **COOPER:** Current personnel — classified. What does that mean?

> **JASON:** It means the system knows something it isn't telling us.

> **CESAR:** Zeke, pull the archive access prompt. Let's see what clearance level it's asking for before we do anything else.

Zeke reached for the input field.

The screen changed.

```
UNAUTHORIZED ESCALATION DETECTED.

INITIATING LOCKDOWN PROTOCOL.

THIS IS NOT A WARNING.
```

---

## IV. DARK

The lights went out.

Not dim. Not flicker. Out.

Every display, every emergency strip, every indicator on every panel — gone. The ventilation — gone. The low background hum of a facility on minimal cycle — confirmed dead, all at once, completely.

Four seconds of absolute dark and absolute silence.

Then:

> **COOPER:** *(somewhere in the black)* Nobody move.

> **JAIME:** I'm here — I've got a light —

Kit lights came on. Three beams finding each other, finding the walls, finding the terminal — dark now, no pulse, no indicator.

> **ZEKE:** Full shutdown. Not protected state. Root-level kill.

> **CESAR:** *(already on comms)* George — status.

A long beat. Then:

> **GEORGE** *(comms — different now)*: We lost everything. Power, displays, dock controls. All of it, same moment. We're on backup air.

> **MARITZA** *(comms, background)*: Mechanical grid is completely dead. I've got nothing to work with.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: What happened in there? The directive said —

> **CESAR:** Robert. Dock release status.

> **ROBERT:** It's — not responding. Lessly —

> **LESSLY** *(comms)*: Dock clamps are locked. I can't release from our side. It has to come from the facility or we need full power to override. We're not going anywhere.

Silence. On both ends.

> **JASON:** *(quietly, to Cesar)* The docking protocol pulled power from our ship trying to handshake. Charge cycle, fuel cell top-off — standard procedure. Except nothing connected on the data side. Power was drawn anyway. Our ship drained.

> **CESAR:** So it wasn't a targeted shutdown.

> **JASON:** The effect is the same. Our ship's dead, clamps are locked, and the facility doesn't have the power to release them. We're not going anywhere until we restore enough of the facility's grid to complete the sequence.

Then — slowly — the emergency grid came back. Lower than before. A pale, cold blue-white trace along the ceiling seams and floor edges. Barely enough to navigate by. The facility breathing again, just barely.

Comms stabilized a moment later.

> **GEORGE** *(comms)*: Low power's back. Air, lights, comms. That's it. Dock release is still locked.

> **MARITZA** *(comms)*: I need the mechanical grid back before I can do anything from here.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Okay. What do we do? Jason — Cesar — what's the move?

> **CESAR:** We find the facility's rear power supply, assess what it needs, and restore enough grid to release the clamps. We can't do that from where you are. It has to come from in here.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: You can do that?

> **JASON:** We can work the problem. That's all I'll tell you right now.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: Right. Okay. Hugo's going to want a —

> **CESAR:** Robert. Stay on channel. Stay calm. We'll check in when we have something.

A pause.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: ...Copy.

> **GEORGE** *(comms — quieter, just to them)*: Hey. You all be careful.

> **CESAR:** We will, George.

---

## V. INTO THE DARK

The low grid ended at the corridor threshold like a boundary someone had drawn on purpose. Beyond it — nothing. The kind of dark that a kit light made a narrow tunnel through and no more.

Cesar went first.

They moved single file. Cesar with his light forward, Jason behind him, Jaime running the map on his kit screen, Zeke at the rear. Cooper fell in between Zeke and Jaime, which put him exactly in the middle.

The corridor ran straight for thirty feet before branching. The facility was bigger than it felt from the outside — high ceilings, walls wide enough for equipment transit. Whatever this place had been built to do, it had been built to last.

> **JAIME:** *(low, checking the map)* Branch left takes us toward Cargo and Engineering. Right runs parallel toward the Crew Quarters section. Straight on is the longer route to the rear grid.

> **CESAR:** Any of those sections flagged on the sensor read?

> **JAIME:** Cargo and Engineering are partial. Crew Quarters didn't register at all.

> **JASON:** That's the section that wasn't on the low grid.

> **CESAR:** We go straight. We don't open doors we don't have to open.

The corridor widened briefly at a transit junction — a lift lobby. The lift doors were closed and dark. A supply cart sat abandoned against the wall, halfway loaded, like someone had stopped mid-task and not come back to finish.

Cooper slowed at it without stopping.

> **COOPER:** *(passing)* Someone left in a hurry.

> **ZEKE:** Or didn't leave at all.

Cooper didn't have a response to that. He kept moving.

Further in, the low grid resumed — faint, intermittent. A door to the left stood open. Panel green. Someone had opened it from inside and it had stayed that way.

Cesar stopped. Looked at it. Looked at Jason.

> **JASON:** Engineering or Maintenance, based on position. Could have what we need to assess the power grid. Could also tell us what happened in here.

> **CESAR:** We go in. Everybody stays in the threshold sightline — nobody moves past the first ten feet until I say.

It was a maintenance bay. Tool racks along the walls, most of them still stocked. A workbench down the center, cleared except for a personal tablet someone had left face-down. One overhead panel had partially collapsed — not from damage, from neglect. Thirty-two years of nothing.

Zeke crossed to the workbench without being asked, picked up the tablet. Tried to power it. Nothing.

> **ZEKE:** Dead. Battery's gone.

> **JAIME:** *(looking at the tool racks)* Most of this is still here. They weren't looting supplies when it happened.

> **JASON:** Whatever it was, it wasn't slow enough to pack.

He moved to the primary systems panel on the far wall. The display was dark but physical indicators remained. One of them was amber.

> **JASON:** This section is on the grid. Low draw, but active. Means the rear power supply is still running something.

> **CESAR:** Can you tell what?

> **JASON:** Not from here. But it's a good sign the supply itself is intact. We find it, there's something to work with.

> **CESAR:** Good enough. Let's keep moving.

> **COOPER:** *(hasn't moved from the threshold)* Are we not going to talk about the fact that the door was already open?

> **CESAR:** It was left open.

> **COOPER:** By who?

A beat.

> **CESAR:** That's what we're trying to find out.

They moved on. Corridor, then a sealed cargo bay they didn't open, then another junction, then a long straight stretch the kit lights couldn't fully reach.

Comms crackled. George.

> **GEORGE** *(comms)*: Still with us?

> **CESAR:** Still moving. Working toward the rear power supply. Nothing to report yet.

> **GEORGE:** We're sitting at backup air and comms. Maritza's been going through the schematics.

> **MARITZA** *(comms, background)*: The rear supply feeds two sub-grids. If you can get either one back up it might be enough to restore dock function. *Might.*

> **JASON:** What does it take to bring a sub-grid back online?

> **MARITZA:** Without knowing what killed it? Could be a manual reset. Could be hardware. Could be something else.

> **JASON:** Could be something else.

> **MARITZA:** That's the one I'd prepare for.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: I want to flag — Hugo's going to ask for a status report when we hit relay range again —

> **CESAR:** Robert, when we have something to report we'll report it.

A pause.

> **ROBERT** *(comms)*: ...Copy.

> **GEORGE** *(comms)*: You'll find it. Go on.

The rear section opened ahead of them — the low grid resuming at full brightness, which meant dim and blue-white and enough to work by. A wide set of pressure doors at the end of the corridor, closed but panel green.

Another door left on open-ready.

> **ZEKE:** *(stopping, just for a moment)* The airlock accepted our override on the first attempt.

Nobody responded immediately.

> **ZEKE:** The Operations Center terminal was in active state. Not sleep. Active. The guest layer came up before I asked for it.

> **JASON:** You're saying it was already running.

> **ZEKE:** I'm saying everything we've touched in here was already on. Not powered up when we arrived. *Already on.* Waiting for input.

A long silence.

> **CESAR:** *(at the pressure doors)* Does that change what we do right now?

> **ZEKE:** No. I just think we should know what we walked into.

> **CESAR:** *(a beat, then)* Noted.

He pressed the panel. The pressure doors split and opened.

## VI. THE POD BAY

The rear section opened into a wider corridor than anything they'd moved through so far — high ceiling, dual-rail infrastructure running overhead, the kind of space built to move heavy equipment. The low grid held here, pale and steady. Doors branched off on either side, most of them sealed.

One wasn't.

Cesar slowed at it without stopping — his standard read of a room before committing to it. The door was open, panel green. Inside, the space dropped two levels and widened into a bay. Structured. Organized. A row of cradle housings along the curved hull wall, each one sized for a six-person pod.

All of them empty.

> **COOPER:** *(quietly)* Those are escape pods.

> **CESAR:** Were.

The cradle housings were intact — the framework, the connection points, the release mechanisms. All of it still there. Just nothing in any of it. Six cradles. Six empty spaces.

> **JAIME:** All of them.

> **ZEKE:** *(already moving toward the wall panel on the far side)* There's a data point.

He pulled it up. The display was dim but functional — local emergency systems, separate power draw. It came alive under his hands.

```
ESCAPE POD BAY — SECTOR 7 / AFT-STARBOARD
CRADLES INSTALLED:      6
PODS DEPLOYED:          6
PODS REMAINING:         0

LAST DEPLOYMENT:        DAY 00000.14 / ALL UNITS
DEPLOYMENT TYPE:        EMERGENCY — UNSCHEDULED

——

VESSEL-WIDE POD STATUS
TOTAL BAYS:             9
TOTAL CAPACITY:         120 PERSONS
PODS DEPLOYED:          MAJORITY — SEE BAY LOGS
PODS REMAINING:         4
REMAINING LOCATION:     BAY 3 / SECTOR 2 / MID-PORT
REMAINING STATUS:       DEGRADED — LAUNCH CAPABILITY UNVERIFIED

——

OUTGOING TRANSMISSION STATUS:    DISABLED
TRANSMISSION DISABLED:           DAY 00000.09
LAST DEPLOYMENT — THIS BAY:      DAY 00000.14

——

EMERGENCY BEACON — ALL PODS:     OFFLINE
REASON:                          TRANSMISSION DISABLE — VESSEL-WIDE
OVERRIDE AVAILABLE:              NO
```

Zeke stood back from it. Nobody said anything immediately.

> **JASON:** The transmissions were cut on day nine. Last pod out of this bay was day fourteen.

> **ZEKE:** Five days between the two. The pods left after the cutoff.

> **JAIME:** So they never had a signal.

> **ZEKE:** They left this vessel completely silent. Whatever beacon the pod had built in — disabled before they were even in the cradle.

Cooper was looking at the empty housings. Not the screen. Just the housings.

> **COOPER:** A hundred and twenty seats. And there are four pods left.

> **CESAR:** Four degraded pods in a section we haven't reached yet. Launch capability unverified.

> **COOPER:** So the math on this is — out of a hundred and twenty, however many actually made it into a pod — they're out there right now. No signal. Thirty-two years of drifting with no signal. And nobody came because nobody could hear them.

The weight of it sat in the room.

> **JAIME:** *(after a moment)* How long do cryo systems hold?

> **JASON:** Depends on the ship. Standard emergency pods — maybe fifty years at full power before the system starts failing. Less if there's any damage.

> **JAIME:** So some of them could still be —

> **JASON:** Yeah.

Another silence.

> **CESAR:** *(measured, but not cold)* We note the pod location. Bay three, mid-port section. We come back to it. Right now we still need the power grid — without that nothing else matters.

He was right and everyone knew it. Didn't make it easier to walk away from the screen.

> **COOPER:** *(not moving yet)* Something cut those transmissions. On purpose. Before the pods were gone.

Nobody answered him. There wasn't an answer yet — not one they had.

> **COOPER:** That's not a malfunction.

> **ZEKE:** No. It isn't.

> **CESAR:** Let's move.

They left the bay. The empty cradles sat behind them in the low light, each one exactly the right size for six people who'd already gone.

Somewhere in the vessel's mid-port section, four degraded pods sat in the dark. The crew didn't know that section yet. They'd have to find it.

Ahead of them — the rear power supply. The dock clamps. Their ship.

One thing at a time.

Comms crackled as they cleared the bay corridor.

> **GEORGE** *(comms — low, steady)*: Still with us?

> **CESAR:** Still moving. Found something. Tell you when we're out.

> **GEORGE:** Copy.

He didn't push. George never pushed.
